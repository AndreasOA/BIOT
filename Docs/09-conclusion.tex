\chapter{Conclusion and Outlook}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This thesis investigated the integration of \ac{xLSTM} architectures~\cite{beck2024xlstm} into the \ac{BIOT} framework~\cite{yang2023biot} for automated \ac{EEG} event classification using the \ac{TUEV} dataset~\cite{harati2015improved,obeid2016temple,ferrell2022electrode}. The study evaluated scalar-memory, matrix-memory, and combined variants across three temporal windows, providing a systematic assessment of architectural behaviour under controlled experimental settings.

\section{Summary of Findings}

The results show that architectural sophistication does not automatically translate into superior biosignal performance. Instead, benefits are strongly dependent on temporal configuration. The \ac{sLSTM} performs best at short and long windows, whereas \ac{mLSTM} performs best at intermediate windows. The Linear Transformer baseline remains competitive at shorter windows but degrades with increasing context length, consistent with known challenges in long-sequence processing~\cite{katharopoulos2020transformers}. These effects must be interpreted in the context of \ac{BIOT} tokenization, which produces relatively short discrete token sequences~\cite{yang2023biot}, reducing reliance on extended long-range memory.

Statistical analysis confirms that most advantages are not significant at the current scale of evaluation, and inter-run variability remains substantial. This indicates that dataset characteristics, noise, and optimization dynamics influence performance at least as strongly as architectural design, echoing broader findings in \ac{EEG} machine learning research~\cite{banville2022robust}.

\section{Clinical and Practical Implications}

From a clinical perspective, class imbalance remains the key limiting factor. Minority classes retain insufficient recall despite architectural changes, highlighting the need for dedicated imbalance mitigation techniques~\cite{cui2019class,lin2017focal}. Without such strategies, none of the tested architectures can be considered clinically reliable.

Regarding computational efficiency, the observed $2.3$--$3.4\times$ longer training time for \ac{xLSTM} variants contrasts with the original paper's emphasis on parallelizability and favorable scaling~\cite{beck2024xlstm}. This discrepancy likely stems from implementation limitations in the \texttt{xlstm} Python package (\url{https://github.com/NX-AI/xlstm/issues/58}) rather than inherent architectural inefficiency, suggesting that runtime should not be considered a definitive architectural limitation.

\section{Key Contributions}

This work contributes:
\begin{itemize}
\item A systematic, window-dependent characterization of \ac{xLSTM} behaviour within \ac{BIOT}.
\item Evidence that performance variation is strongly influenced by temporal configuration and stochastic training effects rather than architecture alone.
\item Transparent reporting of both strengths and limitations, supporting realistic expectations regarding advanced sequence models in biosignal processing.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}

The main limitations are limited statistical power due to five seeds per configuration, evaluation on a single imbalanced dataset~\cite{harati2015improved,obeid2016temple}, shared hyperparameters across architectures, and restricted exploration of temporal windows.

\section{Future Directions}

Future work should prioritise:
\begin{itemize}
\item Architecture-specific hyperparameter optimization.
\item Class imbalance mitigation (e.g., focal loss, class-balanced loss, SMOTE, ADASYN~\cite{cui2019class,lin2017focal,chawla2002smote,he2008adasyn}).
\item Cross-dataset validation.
\item More detailed exploration of temporal context and potentially adaptive or multi-scale temporal modeling.
\end{itemize}

\section{Final Reflection}

Overall, this thesis demonstrates that while \ac{xLSTM} architectures offer promising modeling capabilities, their benefits in biosignal processing are nuanced and context-dependent rather than universally superior. Reliable \ac{EEG} classification requires a combined focus on representation design, statistical robustness, and clinical realism. Rather than discouraging further exploration, these findings establish a grounded foundation for future research aimed at bridging methodological innovation and clinically meaningful impact.
